SHARK COURSE - QUICK PROJECT GUIDE
Updated: 2026-10-15

GOAL
Build a playable Unity course: start platform -> five moving shark platforms -> finish platform. Sharks are calm until boarded; they bite after 2 seconds if the player is still on top. Falling or getting bitten respawns the player at the start. R restarts the course.

WHAT WAS USED
- Unity 6.3 and C# scripts.
- glTFast 6.20.0 package to import the shark GLB model.
- Unity Animator controllers for the shark swim/bite and player animations.
- CharacterController for the course player's movement and jumping.
- Unity Editor/AssetDatabase tools to generate and organize prefabs and rebuild the course while keeping asset GUID references.
- Models and player animations from the supplied shark and FallGuyClone assets; Kenney-style materials for the course.

HOW THE SHARK WORKS
1. The shark has a solid top collider and a separate boarding trigger.
2. A player-controller collision or trigger contact registers a player only when their collider is on the shark's top surface.
3. A 2-second countdown starts. If the player steps off or is no longer alive, the target/countdown clears and no bite damage is applied.
4. When the countdown completes, the shark stops swimming, plays its bite animation, and smoothly pitches its visual upward by 60 degrees. The platform collider stays level.
5. Damage happens only if the same player is still standing on top when the bite lands. The shark then resets so the course can be played again.
6. While swimming, the visual faces 90 degrees to the right or -90 degrees to the left. Sharks move sideways in a repeating path.

COURSE AND PLAYER
- SampleScene has a start platform, five sharks, a finish platform/line, and water.
- The standalone Course Player moves with WASD, jumps with Space, sprints with Shift, and is followed by a smooth mouse-orbit camera.
- On a bite, the receiver plays the player's death animation (when available); the course manager waits, then respawns the player. Falling below the course also respawns them. R resets player and sharks. Crossing the finish line ends the level.
- The player/death receiver is currently this course's standalone implementation; it is not yet connected to the team's shared player/death system.

WHAT TO CHANGE
- Bite delay, pitch, swim range, and swim speed: select a shark or edit SharkPlatform.prefab; settings are on SharkPlatformController.cs.
- Course spacing, number of sharks, start/finish positions, and palette colors: edit SharkCourseBuilder.cs.
- Player speed, jump, and gravity: edit SharkCoursePlayerController.cs.
- Camera feel: edit SmoothPlayerCamera.cs or its camera component settings in the scene.
- Player death integration: replace or connect SharkBiteReceiver.cs to the team's player/death system.

VALIDATION / CLEANUP
- Unity compilation succeeded. A Play Mode check passed for safe step-off, bite timing, direction-facing, stopping during bites, bite/fall respawn, and R restart.
- Only verified-empty folders were removed. Unity's Library cache was left alone because the Editor was open.
- For manual changes, edit the source scripts/prefabs above; the scene can be rebuilt from the Unity Tools > Shark menu.
